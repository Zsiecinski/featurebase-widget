# Loop — cold outreach target list

A working template for tracking the first 20-50 companies you reach out
to on launch day. Goal: get 10+ early installs to seed engagement data,
testimonials, and product-market-fit signal.

## How to find targets

The ideal target uses **both** Featurebase and Intercom. Sources:

1. **Featurebase customer page** — `https://www.featurebase.app/customers`
   has a public list of who they work with. Cross-reference against Intercom.
2. **BuiltWith** — `https://builtwith.com/relationships/intercom.com`
   shows sites running Intercom. Manually check which also use Featurebase.
3. **Twitter / LinkedIn** — search `"using Featurebase"` or `"on Intercom"`
   for founders mentioning both.
4. **Indie Hackers founder directory** — most IH founders share their stack.
   Filter for SaaS with active changelog needs.
5. **Personal network** — anyone you know who runs CS for a SaaS company is
   a fit, even if they're not on Featurebase yet (they'll switch once they
   see Loop).

## Outreach playbook

### Day 0 (launch)
- Send first 10 cold emails (highest-confidence targets)
- DM 3-5 founder friends in your personal network
- Don't blast — 1-1 personalized is 10x more effective

### Day 3
- Send 10 more emails to next-tier targets
- Follow up with non-responders from Day 0 (one short bump only)

### Day 7
- Last batch of cold emails (cap at 50 total to avoid spam-mode)
- Don't follow up a third time on no-replies

### Day 14
- Interview the first 5 installs (Calendly link in welcome email)
- Ask: what almost stopped you installing? what would you pay for?

## Tracking what worked

Add a column for **source** in your spreadsheet (`Twitter DM`, `Cold email`,
`IH comment`, `Reddit DM`, etc.) so you can attribute installs back to
the channel that drove them.

After 30 days, you'll know which channel delivers paying customers vs.
free triers. Double down on the winners for the next launch cycle.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- ❌ Bulk-sending a generic template — Intercom and Featurebase users
  will recognize a mass email and ignore
- ❌ Following up more than once after the initial bump
- ❌ Pretending you have customers you don't
- ❌ Cold-emailing companies with no public changelog — they probably
  don't see value in Loop yet
- ❌ Sending the same template to founders and CS managers — adjust
  tone (founders care about deflection, CS managers care about
  ticket volume)
