Loop vs. linking to your changelog

You could just paste your Featurebase URL into Intercom's Messenger as a help link. Most teams already do. Here's why a Canvas Kit card outperforms a link in every metric we've measured.

Three scenarios where Loop wins decisively

1. The "is this fixed yet?" ticket

Customer hits a bug, you ship the fix two weeks later, customer doesn't know. They open Intercom: "Is the size chart bug fixed?"

"With a link to our changelog, they'd never find that out. With Loop, the first thing they see when they open the Messenger is 'FIXED · Size chart bug · 3 days ago.' Half those tickets vanish before they're typed."

2. The "are you ever going to add X?" question

You have X on your roadmap. Customer doesn't know. They ask, you respond, they wait, you ship — but they've already churned to a competitor.

"Loop's Coming Next section tells them X is coming, before they ask. Customers who see active development on what they care about don't churn."

3. The "I had no idea you shipped that!" missed-value moment

You launched a major feature. You posted about it on Twitter, sent an email blast, updated your docs. Most existing customers still don't know.

"Email open rates are ~30%. Loop's render rate is ~100% — every customer who opens the Messenger sees what you shipped. It's the most reliable surface to announce wins."

When linking is actually fine

We're not going to pretend Loop is always the right answer. Stick with a link if:

  • You publish to your changelog less than once a month — there's not enough fresh content to warrant a card
  • Your Intercom plan doesn't support Canvas Kit Messenger apps (rare, but a few legacy plans don't)
  • Your changelog content isn't customer-facing — it's internal release notes for engineers

For everyone else: Loop deflects "is this fixed?" tickets, drives upvotes on your roadmap, and turns the Messenger into your highest-converting changelog surface.
